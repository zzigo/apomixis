# Apomixis
## Performance synopsis for the score

*Apomixis* should be performed as an autonomous proliferating system. The human performer does not "conduct" the machine, but enters an already active field, perturbing it from within. The organ behaves as a reproductive automaton: it clones, drifts, accumulates, inhibits, densifies, and filters itself. Human intervention appears only in four pieces, each one corresponding to a different degree of *anthropomixis*.

The performer should avoid Romantic rhetoric unless explicitly indicated. Articulation should remain precise, modal, and behavior-based rather than phrase-based. Think in terms of vectors, strata, thresholds, and pressure zones. Silence, waiting, and non-action are active musical states.

## Human intervention points

### 03. Meiosis / Flower

**Mode:** 50/50 shared control between performer and system.

- `Introduction`
  - No human intervention.
  - Allow the system to establish its initial reduction loop alone.
- `Replication`
  - First intervention window.
  - Enter with short modal cells, parallel intervals, or restrained fauxbourdon.
  - Stay rhythmically strict; do not impose rubato.
- `Bridge`
  - Second intervention window.
  - Fragment the material into hocket-like exchanges, broken dyads, or short isorhythmic puncta.
- `Climax`
  - Third intervention window.
  - Increase pressure through denser parallel motion, cadential compression, or registral widening.
  - Cut off decisively rather than fading sentimentally.
- `Coda`
  - Return to non-intervention unless specifically notated otherwise.

### 05. Polygonum / Embryo sac

**Mode:** guided free improvisation across the full five-part form.

- `Introduction`
  - State a bare modal axis, dyad, or slow organum-like contour.
- `Replication`
  - Expand the material using medieval and Renaissance-derived behaviors:
  - organum in parallel or oblique motion;
  - fauxbourdon;
  - hocket;
  - diminutions;
  - sustained tenors with moving upper strands.
- `Bridge`
  - Thin out the activity.
  - Act as if entering an already self-regulating chamber rather than taking the foreground.
- `Climax`
  - Intensify through accumulation, registral expansion, and cadential pressure.
  - The performer should energize the system, not dominate it.
- `Coda`
  - Withdraw toward interiority, as though the chamber closes again around the human trace.

### 06. Oosfera

**Mode:** no more than three punctual human events.

- `Event 1`
  - At the end of the `Introduction`.
  - A brief activation: a single sonority, attack, interval, or pulse that awakens the chamber.
- `Event 2`
  - In the `Bridge`.
  - A second, equally brief event that redirects the active layers or opens a new register zone.
- `Event 3`
  - In the late `Coda` or immediately after the `Climax` break, depending on the score version.
  - This event seals or suspends the process rather than decorating it.

Each of the three events should be brief, dry, unambiguous, and structurally decisive.

### 09. Microspora / Pollen grain

**Mode:** final piece; voice with ornamental pitch-following.

- `Introduction`
  - No human sound until the machine loop is stable.
- `Replication`
  - The voice enters softly, close to speech, following prosody more than lyric line.
  - The vocal material may be pitched, whispered, breathed, half-sung, or phonemic, depending on the notation.
- `Bridge`
  - The system shadows, ornaments, or parasitizes the voice.
  - The performer should remain calm and unforced; the machine is not accompanying, but infesting the vocal contour.
- `Climax`
  - A stronger projection is possible, but never operatic.
  - The voice should sound embedded in the instrument, not placed in front of it as a soloist.
- `Coda`
  - Reduce gradually toward breath, phoneme, residue, or near-whisper.
  - The cycle ends by re-humanizing the automaton from inside.

If the voice is produced by the organist, it should feel like an internal leak of the machine. If a second performer is used, they should remain visually integrated into the apparatus rather than isolated as a front-stage soloist.

## Lighting suggestions

Lighting should not illustrate botany. The room should read as a germination chamber, a reproductive mechanism, or an interior ecosystem.

### General light behavior

- Keep most transitions slow and structural rather than expressive.
- Favor side light, low front light, and dim ambient hall states.
- Avoid spectacular color changes unless tied to a formal threshold.
- Let darkness remain active; visibility should feel earned, not default.

### Suggested states

- `Automata-only pieces`
  - Cool or neutral temperature.
  - Low side light.
  - Audience mostly in shadow.
  - Hall glow around `5% to 12%`.
- `Anthropomixis entries`
  - A subtle warm lift when the human enters.
  - Not a spotlight reveal, but a slight increase of bodily presence.
  - Hall glow around `12% to 20%`.
- `04. Megaspora`
  - Near-darkness.
  - Very slow breathing dimmer curves.
  - Visual sensation of suspended interior growth.
- `07. Filotaxis`
  - Brighter and sharper than the rest of the cycle.
  - Crisp transitions.
  - Clear geometric definition rather than atmosphere.
- `09. Microspora`
  - Warm, narrow focus on the vocal source and/or console.
  - The rest of the room should slowly recede.
  - In the final minute, reduce the hall almost completely, leaving only a residual halo or thin amber-white trace.

## Short version for inclusion in the score

*Apomixis* is conceived as an autonomous musical reproductive system. The performer enters this system only at selected thresholds, not as a traditional soloist but as a local perturbation within an already active automaton. Human intervention appears in four pieces: *Meiosis* (shared control), *Polygonum* (guided improvisation), *Oosfera* (three punctual actions), and *Microspora* (voice with ornamental pitch-following, closing the cycle). Lighting should remain structural, dim, and non-illustrative, treating the hall as an interior chamber of germination rather than a theatrical backdrop.
